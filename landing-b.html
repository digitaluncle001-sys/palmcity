Land has always meant something different here

In much of Nigeria, land is the one asset still spoken of in generations rather than years. It is what a father points to and says, "this will still be here for your children."

The trouble is that land on its own is a patient asset. It appreciates, but it does not provide for anyone while it waits.

"What if the land your family holds for the next generation was also feeding the household in this one?"

An oil-palm tree does not need to choose between the two. It can appreciate as land and pay as an asset, in the same year, on the same plot.

Why the timeline matters more than the yield

Oil-palm trees are productive for 25 to 30 years. The land can be replanted and brought back into production once a cycle ends. That is not small print — it is the entire point.

A single decision made now can still be producing income for a child who has not been born yet, or a grandchild who never meets you.

The problem with dividing land among children

The familiar route: land is bought, held, then divided into smaller and less useful pieces for each child. Palm City does not need to be divided to be shared. Income from an operating estate can be distributed; the underlying asset stays intact.
